**Lunka** never wields a sword or casts a hex, yet almost everything **[Coen](/characters/coen/)** does is for her. She is his sister — and her sickness is the spark that lights the entire story.

## The plague that starts everything

Lunka falls gravely ill with the **plague** sweeping Vale Sangora. With no cure within a peasant family's reach, she is dying — until the vampire lord **[Brencis](/characters/brencis/)** intervenes and **saves her with his own blood**.

It is not a gift. Brencis immediately turns that mercy into **leverage over Coen's family**, effectively holding them hostage and setting the roughly **30-day** clock that frames the adventure. (For how that timer actually works, see our [30-day time limit explainer](/guides/30-day-time-limit-explained/).)

## Why she matters to the player

Lunka grounds the stakes. The game isn't asking you to save the world — it's asking you to save *her*, and your mother **[Esme](/characters/esme/)**, in the time you have left. Every choice about how far Coen leans into his vampire nature carries the weight of who he's doing it for.

That personal scale is a big part of what makes Dawnwalker's story feel different; we dig into it in the [Story, Setting & Characters guide](/guides/story-setting-characters/).
