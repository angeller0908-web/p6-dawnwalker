Nearly two weeks after the highly anticipated release of Rebel Wolves' dark fantasy epic, *The Blood of Dawnwalker*, the dust has settled on the initial wave of reviews and day-one guides. Millions of players are now deeply entrenched in the grim, blood-soaked world of Vale Sangora. As the community moves past the honeymoon phase and into multiple playthroughs and endgame min-maxing, a distinct post-launch meta is beginning to form on social media and dedicated forums.

Currently, the player base is intensely focused on dissecting the game's brutal combat mechanics, debating the controversial narrative pacing, and hunting down easily missable prologue secrets. At the forefront of community discussions is the widespread praise for the game's "Duelist" difficulty setting—a mode that completely reimagines how action-RPG difficulty scaling should function in modern titles. Conversely, while the moment-to-moment combat is receiving universal acclaim, the game's overarching 30-day time limit is proving highly polarizing, leading to a vocal segment of the player base petitioning Rebel Wolves for a relaxed, no-time-limit mode in future updates.

In this community status report, we are examining why the Duelist difficulty is being hailed as a mechanical masterclass, looking at the ongoing debate surrounding the game's ticking clock, and highlighting a few crucial early-game secrets—such as the exact trigger for Anca's romance—that you might have missed on your first run.

## Why 'Duelist' Difficulty is a Masterclass in ARPG Design

When it comes to difficulty settings in massive open-world RPGs, developers often fall into a predictable trap: the "bullet sponge" effect. Typically, raising the difficulty setting merely inflates enemy health pools and artificially boosts their damage output. This lazy approach forces players to execute the exact same combat loops, just for a painstakingly longer duration while punishing minor mistakes with instant death. It is artificial difficulty at its most tedious.

*The Blood of Dawnwalker* takes a radically different, highly player-friendly approach with its "Duelist" difficulty, and the community is taking rigorous notes. As highlighted by players across social media platforms like Reddit—with fans even urging other franchises like *The Elder Scrolls* to adopt similar systems—the Duelist mode abandons the traditional stat-bloat philosophy entirely. Instead of bumping up enemy health pools, this hardcore setting actually lessens them. 

To balance this reduction in enemy health, the game completely removes all visual attack indicators. You will no longer see flashes or UI warnings when an enemy is about to strike; instead, players must rely purely on reading enemy animation wind-ups and environmental cues to successfully parry or dodge. Furthermore, the Duelist setting makes the protagonist hit significantly harder, but exacts a much heavier toll on stamina consumption for every swing, dodge, and hex cast. 

The result is a hyper-lethal, fast-paced dance of death where both you and your enemies can be dispatched in just a few well-placed strikes. It removes the artificial padding of spongy combat and replaces it with a genuinely skill-based difficulty curve. This design choice dramatically deepens the roleplaying experience. By making combat highly lethal, it alters the power curve of the entire playthrough. Every encounter feels dangerous, and proper preparation, positioning, and stamina management become paramount for survival. 

Fans of hardcore modding experiences have pointed out that the Duelist mode captures a highly sought-after feeling, delivering immensely rewarding gameplay without the frustration of whittling down bloated health bars. If you are starting a second playthrough, engaging with the Duelist mode is highly recommended to fully appreciate the combat system. ## The 30-Day Time Limit Debate and the Push for a "No-Time-Limit" Mode

While the moment-to-moment combat is thriving, the macro-structure of the game is currently the subject of fierce debate. *The Blood of Dawnwalker* features a strict 30-day in-game time limit to complete your primary objectives and stave off a catastrophic event. This ticking clock is deeply woven into the narrative, adding immense weight to your choices, travel times, and even dictating when you decide to rest to heal your wounds. If you are struggling to understand how this system tracks your progress, refer to our guide where the [30-day time limit is explained](/guides/30-day-time-limit-explained/).

However, a growing faction within the community is finding this constant pressure antithetical to the open-world sandbox experience they desire. On community forums, players have begun petitioning Rebel Wolves to introduce an optional "no-time-limit" mode—or at least a relaxed mode where the timer is paused indefinitely. The core argument is that Vale Sangora is packed with rich lore, intricate side quests, and stunning vistas, but players feel too anxious to engage with this optional content when they know the clock is constantly ticking against them. 

For many, the desire is simply to take their time, immerse themselves in the world, and clear out the map without the looming threat of a fail state. Alongside this, there are heavy requests for expanded HUD customization. Currently, players are unable to hide certain UI elements. Being able to toggle off the visual reminders of the time limit might serve as a psychological band-aid for those feeling overwhelmed by the urgency of the main quest.

Will Rebel Wolves compromise their original artistic vision to add a timer-free mode? The studio has explicitly stated in past interviews that the timer is fundamental to the game's stakes and is heavily tied to the multiple epilogues. Removing it entirely would likely break several quest triggers and narrative sequences. However, they might explore a post-game free-roam state or a "story mode" variant that drastically slows down the passage of time. Until an official statement is made, players will have to learn to prioritize their objectives efficiently.

## Community Mods and Hopes for PS5 Pro Enhancements

While console players are waiting for official patches to address quality-of-life requests, the PC modding scene for *The Blood of Dawnwalker* is already in full swing. Because the game relies heavily on the dichotomy between the protagonist's human form during the day and their monstrous vampire form at night, the gameplay loops are strictly segregated by the sun. 

Naturally, modders have already dismantled these boundaries. Recent community-reported mods have surfaced that allow players to access overpowered vampire abilities while remaining in their daytime human form. While this completely breaks the intended balance of the game—and negates the tension of being caught out during the day without your full arsenal—it offers a chaotic, power-fantasy sandbox experience for those who have already rolled the credits. Fans are hoping that Rebel Wolves will utilize upgraded hardware capabilities in the future to offer better image quality, higher resolution targets, improved ray-traced reflections in the rain-slicked streets of Vale Sangora, and a rock-solid frame rate during the most visually demanding combat sequences. 

## Early Game Secrets: Triggering the Anca Romance and Finding Contraband

Whether you are holding out for a performance patch, waiting to see if a no-time-limit mode manifests, or diving back in on the Duelist difficulty, there are several easily missed early-game interactions that are currently trending in community search queries.

One of the most heavily searched topics right now is how to initiate the romance with Anca, the enigmatic witch. Because *The Blood of Dawnwalker* does not explicitly signpost relationship mechanics or approval ratings, many players have accidentally locked themselves out of her storyline by rushing through the opening hours. 

To successfully start Anca's romance, you must act decisively during the game's prologue. You will be tasked with purchasing herbs specifically for Coen's mother. Choosing to buy these medicinal herbs directly from Anca triggers a unique narrative sequence: a severe storm will arrive shortly after, and Anca will suggest that you stay and study with her while the weather passes. Agreeing to this impromptu study date is the mandatory first step to locking in her romance path, which eventually spans multiple quests later in the game. If you miss this in the prologue, the window closes. For a complete list of relationship paths, consult our extensive [romance guide](/guides/romance-guide/).

Beyond relationships, players are aggressively hunting for late-game advantages hidden in the early hours. Guides detailing where to find Silver Contraband are highly sought after, as this rare resource is reportedly crucial for upgrading high-tier hex magic and crafting specialized anti-monster weaponry. Similarly, the mechanics surrounding the management of Blood Hunger and the exact steps required to unlock the devastating Soul Reaping ability are dominating community forums as players look to optimize their builds. 

Mastering these systems early on is especially critical if you are attempting to survive the grueling Duelist difficulty. Rebel Wolves has crafted an incredibly dense world where actions have permanent consequences. From the lethal precision required in combat to the meticulous planning needed to beat the 30-day clock, *The Blood of Dawnwalker* continues to captivate its audience, demanding attention to detail at every turn. As the developers gather this early feedback from the player base, it will be fascinating to see how the game evolves in the coming months.
