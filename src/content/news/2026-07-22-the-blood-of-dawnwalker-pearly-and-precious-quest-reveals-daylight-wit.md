Rebel Wolves continues to pull back the curtain on their highly anticipated dark fantasy RPG, offering us our most detailed look yet at how the game handles its daytime gameplay loop. The latest exclusive footage showcases an entire side quest titled "Pearly and Precious," shedding significant light on the stark differences between playing as a creature of the night and fighting as a human during the day. 

For months, the community has heavily debated how the day-and-night cycle would actually impact character builds and combat encounters. This new footage gives us concrete answers, introducing us to daylight witchcraft, intense sword-on-sword dueling, and a macabre new economy involving a creature known as a Sanzhana.

Here is our complete breakdown of the new mechanics shown in the "Pearly and Precious" quest, and what they mean for your journey through Vale Sangora.

## Surviving the Daylight: Witchcraft Over Vampire Skills

Up until now, much of the marketing for the game has focused heavily on the visceral, supernatural abilities available when the sun goes down. However, the "Pearly and Precious" quest is explicitly set during the daytime. As previously hinted by the developers, protagonist Coen cannot rely on his vampiric skills when the sun is up. Instead, he must lean heavily on human weaponry and, more importantly, witchcraft.

The gameplay footage provides our first clear look at how witchcraft functions in real-time combat. Unlike the explosive, blood-fueled powers of the night, daylight magic appears highly tactical. We see Coen utilizing a specific speed-enhancing spell. While the exact duration and stamina cost remain unconfirmed, this mobility buff seems absolutely essential for closing the gap against ranged enemies or dodging heavy melee strikes when you lack supernatural reflexes.

The centerpiece of the magical showcase, however, is a spell called the **Ravenous Flock**. This offensive curse triggers a violent eruption of spectral birds that swarm and damage all nearby hostile targets. From the footage, it functions as an excellent crowd-control area-of-effect (AoE) ability. Whenever Coen is surrounded by bandits, the Ravenous Flock interrupts enemy attack animations and provides enough breathing room to reposition. 

This creates a fascinating dynamic for players mapping out their [Builds and Skill Trees](/guides/builds-and-skill-trees/). If your progression heavily favors nocturnal powers, you might find yourself severely disadvantaged during daytime ambushes. Investing points into witchcraft curses like the Ravenous Flock is expected to be a mandatory survival strategy rather than a purely optional playstyle. It forces a dual-build mentality where players must balance their point allocation between day and night proficiencies.

## The Brutality of Sword-on-Sword Dueling

When Coen is not casting spectral birds, he is crossing blades. The quest footage features extensive sword-on-sword dueling, heavily grounding the combat in grim reality. Without the raw physical strength of his vampire form, Coen's daytime melee encounters require precise timing, parrying, and positioning. 

The clashes of steel look weighty and methodical. Enemies do not simply stand around waiting to be hit; they actively block and attempt to flank. This aligns perfectly with the development team's previous remarks comparing the rhythm of their swordplay to musical action games. You have to find the correct tempo to break an opponent's guard. If you are interested in a deeper look at the underlying mechanics of these encounters, check out our dedicated analysis on [Combat and Hex Magic](/guides/combat-and-hex-magic/). 

It is highly likely that daytime combat will push players to rely more on their crafted gear, blade oils, and physical armor ratings. Without the passive health regeneration that is reportedly tied to the vampire form at night, taking a stray sword hit during the day carries much more consequence. 

## Meet the Sanzhana: A Macabre Economy

Beyond the combat, the "Pearly and Precious" quest introduces a fascinating new NPC and economic mechanic. The quest revolves around a creature called a Sanzhana, colloquially referred to in the world as a "pixie." 

If you have been keeping up with the expanding lore regarding the [Vale Sangora World](/guides/vale-sangora-world-guide/), you know that Rebel Wolves does not do traditional, lighthearted fantasy. True to the studio's dark fantasy roots, this pixie is not a helpful fairy offering free guidance. The Sanzhana has items of "great value" to trade, but it only accepts a very specific, gruesome currency: human teeth.

This leads directly to one of the most unsettling interactive mechanics shown so far. After winning a brutal skirmish against human enemies, the footage shows Coen looting the bodies. However, he is not just checking their pockets for coins or ammunition. He is explicitly shown poking around in the mouths of the corpses to extract their teeth. 

This mechanic is brilliant from a ludonarrative perspective. It forces the player to engage in a highly morbid act just to participate in this specific trade economy. It blurs the line between Coen's humanity and his monstrous nature. Even when playing during the day, ostensibly in his "human" form, you are still acting like a predator scavenging remains.

### What Treasures Do Teeth Buy?

The exact inventory of the Sanzhana remains a mystery, though the footage promises that the creature offers something of great value. The community is already theorizing heavily about what this could mean. 

It is reportedly tied to character progression. Some players speculate that Sanzhana vendors might be the exclusive source for rare witchcraft grimoires, allowing you to unlock higher-tier daytime spells. Others believe the teeth might be traded for unique crafting materials necessary to upgrade specific silver weapons. 

Given that the developer has confirmed a highly reactive world, we also have to wonder if mutilating corpses to collect teeth will have consequences. Will NPCs or local guards react aggressively if they catch you prying teeth from the dead? The [Infamy System](/guides/infamy-system-explained/) is expected to track your moral transgressions, and looting mouths feels like an action that would certainly raise some eyebrows in civilized settlements. 

## Structuring the Narrative Sandbox

"Pearly and Precious" is described as a relatively short errand, yet it manages to introduce an entirely new creature, a specific magic system, and a unique vendor currency. This speaks volumes about the density of the narrative sandbox Rebel Wolves is building. 

Rather than dumping these mechanics on the player via tutorial pop-ups, the game integrates them organically into minor quests. You meet the pixie, you learn what it wants, you fight some bandits, you harvest the currency, and you make a trade. It is a seamless way to teach the player how the world operates without breaking immersion. 

As we inch closer to the confirmed September 3 launch, seeing an uninterrupted, start-to-finish quest is incredibly reassuring. It shows that the team is confident in their core gameplay loop. The transition between exploration, dialogue, combat, and looting appears fluid. For those curious about how quests like this fit into the broader narrative structure—especially considering the game's ambitious multiple endings—you can read more about how choice impacts the world in our guide on [Story, Setting, and Characters](/guides/story-setting-characters/).

## Preparing for the Hunt

The reveal of the "Pearly and Precious" quest gives us a lot of homework to do before the game drops. Understanding the stark contrast between daytime vulnerability and nocturnal power is clearly going to be the difference between life and death in Vale Sangora. 

Players will need to map out their strategies carefully. Will you invest heavily in witchcraft curses like the Ravenous Flock to ensure you are never caught off guard during the day? Or will you save your skill points for your [Vampire Powers and Wolf Form](/guides/vampire-powers-and-wolf-form/), opting to simply hide or flee when the sun is up? Furthermore, are you willing to desecrate the dead to appease a pixie, or will you walk away from the Sanzhana's treasures to keep your hands—and conscience—relatively clean?
