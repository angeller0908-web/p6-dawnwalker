The launch of *The Blood of Dawnwalker* has firmly established Rebel Wolves' dark fantasy RPG as a massive time sink, despite its much-discussed in-game chronological limits. Over a week into release, players pushing past the [prologue and Laslea Glen](/guides/2026-09-03-the-blood-of-dawnwalker-prologue-walkthrough-laslea-glen-quests-contro/) are colliding with some of the game's most unforgiving encounters and morally ambiguous quest lines. If you are struggling with your build economy, debating faction loyalties, or getting absolutely walled by the endgame dungeons on higher difficulties, we have compiled the definitive strategies to push Coen through the back half of the campaign.

## Navigating the "Hive and Seek" Quest Choices

One of the more polarizing mid-game side stories is the "Hive and Seek" quest. While venturing into the subterranean cavern networks of Vale Sangora with Lacra, you will eventually intercept a group known as The Hive goblins. They present a clear proposition: help them deal with a formidable target known as Chonk, or refuse their plea entirely. 

This decision fundamentally alters how you traverse the underground biomes for the rest of your playthrough.

**Helping The Hive with Chonk:**
Choosing to assist the goblins is mechanically demanding but highly rewarding. Chonk operates similarly to a heavy brute boss, requiring you to carefully manage your stamina and rely heavily on directional parries. Since this fight typically occurs in a poorly lit environment, it is highly recommended to tackle this objective during Coen's nighttime vampire form. Activating your Blood Surge allows you to absorb Chonk's wide area-of-effect slams, while utilizing hex magic to strip away his physical armor plating will make the fight significantly shorter. 

Successfully defeating Chonk reportedly grants you permanent safe passage through Hive-controlled territories. Furthermore, the goblins will open up a unique trading inventory containing rare crafting materials vital for upgrading late-game armor sets, saving you days of grinding.

**Refusing The Hive:**
Walking away sounds like the safer narrative option, aligning with a more stoic, non-interventionist approach to Coen's morality. However, doing so immediately flags The Hive as hostile for the remainder of the game. Every time you enter the lower caverns, you will face persistent ambushes. While this provides a steady farm for basic experience points, the constant attrition drains your healing resources and eats into your overarching time limit. Unless you are intentionally farming for specific enemy drops, refusing the quest creates unnecessary friction that bogs down your exploration.

## The "Song of the Mountain" Confrontation

Further escalating the faction tensions is the "Song of the Mountain" questline. Approaching the sacred alpine peaks brings Coen face-to-face with the Uriash guards. The game forces a hard choice here: fight your way through the blockade or find a way to spare them and enter peacefully.

**Fighting the Uriash Guards:**
If you choose violence, expect a grueling gauntlet. The Uriash utilize shield-wall tactics that completely shut down light attacks. If you are playing during the daytime in human form, you will need to rely on heavy, charged strikes to break their guard, followed immediately by defensive dodges to avoid their synchronized counter-attacks. Wiping out the guards severely damages your reputation with the mountain clans, locking you out of several localized side quests and vendor discounts. On the flip side, they drop high-tier weaponry that can easily carry you through the next several boss encounters without needing to visit a blacksmith.

**Sparing the Uriash:**
Entering peacefully requires you to have invested heavily in the persuasion and dialogue branches of the skill tree, or finding a hidden traversal route along the cliffside. Sneaking past them utilizing the environment—or talking your way through—preserves your clan standing. Given the brutal difficulty spikes later in the game, maintaining friendly outposts where you can rest and restock without penalty is usually the smarter tactical play for a first playthrough. 

## Surviving Bakir's Maze on Duellist Difficulty

The community consensus is clear: Bakir’s Maze is the ultimate skill check in *The Blood of Dawnwalker*, particularly if you are playing on the punishing Duellist difficulty setting. Several players on the achievement forums have cited this specific dungeon as the most horrendous, controller-breaking segment of the entire RPG. 

Bakir's Maze strips away your ability to rely on brute force. The corridors are narrow, the camera angles can occasionally fight against you, and the enemies attack with hyper-aggressive tracking. 

To survive the Maze on Duellist, you need to rethink your approach to combat:
- **Abandon the Heavy Swings:** The tight geometry means your wide-sweeping heavy attacks will frequently bounce off the walls, leaving you staggered and open to fatal counter-hits. Equip a thrust-focused weapon—like a rapier or a standard short sword—and prioritize quick, linear jabs.
- **Abuse Hex Magic:** Area-denial hexes are your best friend here. Drop lingering damage-over-time spells in chokepoints and let the enemies walk to you. Do not chase them into open rooms where you can be easily surrounded by ranged attackers.
- **Time Your Transformations:** You want to enter the Maze just as the sun is setting. Navigating the traps in human form is slightly easier due to the slower, more deliberate movement mechanics. But the moment you hit the combat arenas, you need the raw speed and life-leeching capabilities of the vampire form to survive the mob encounters. If you mismanage the clock and enter during the day, your lack of Blood Surge will make the final chamber nearly impossible.

## Talent Point Optimization and the Infinite Money Glitch

Before launch, there was widespread concern that unlocking all the nodes in the skill trees would require a dedicated New Game Plus run. After nearly two weeks of extensive playtime, trophy hunters have confirmed that this is entirely false.

The game's economy of talent points is incredibly generous if you actually engage with the side content. By completing major side quests, clearing out guard checkpoints, and participating in dynamic world events, players are reaching the end of the campaign with anywhere from 60 to 80 unspent talent points. You absolutely do not need to horde your points or meticulously plan a singular "perfect" build. You can afford to max out the core swordplay tree, heavily invest in hex magic, and still have plenty of points left over for utility skills like increased carrying capacity or extended dialogue options. 

If you are looking to expedite your progression even further, you should take advantage of the currently active infinite money glitch. As reported heavily by the community over the weekend, certain merchants in the central hub possess a bugged inventory refresh rate. By selling a high-value item, immediately closing the dialogue window, and reopening it during a specific frame window, you can buy the item back for zero cost and resell it. This allows you to generate infinite currency, which can then be used to buy out all the available skill books and crafting materials. Be warned: Rebel Wolves has already deployed Hotfix 1.0.5, and while the glitch survived that minor patch, it is highly likely to be squashed in the upcoming major title update. Utilize the market exploit while the window remains open.

## The PC Modding Scene Rewrites the Rules

While console players are navigating the game as Rebel Wolves intended, the PC community is rapidly stripping away the game's core restrictions. If you are playing on Steam, the modding scene has already delivered features that completely alter the intended experience, pushing the boundaries of the engine.

**Removing the 30-Day Time Limit:**
The overarching chronological limit has been a point of massive contention. While [our previous coverage explained why the time limit is manageable](/guides/30-day-time-limit-explained/), many players simply hate being on a clock in their massive RPGs. A recently released mod completely freezes the calendar, allowing you to explore Vale Sangora endlessly, grind out every single mob, and complete every minor fetch quest without the looming threat of a forced game over. 

**New Game Plus and Bat Flight:**
Officially, *The Blood of Dawnwalker* does not feature a New Game Plus mode at launch, though developers have hinted it might arrive in a future update. Modders couldn't wait. A popular script now allows you to carry over your fully upgraded Coen into a fresh save file, scaling enemy health and damage to match your endgame level.

Even more impressive is the introduction of a custom Bat Flight mechanic. In the vanilla game, Coen’s vampire form grants incredible agility and leaping distance, but true flight is restricted to cinematic cutscenes. Modders have cracked the movement physics, allowing players to physically transform into a bat and fly over the map's geography. It completely breaks the intended level design and puzzle platforming, but for those who just want the ultimate vampire power fantasy, it is an essential download that fundamentally changes how you interact with the verticality of Vale Sangora.

As *The Blood of Dawnwalker* continues to evolve through official patches and community-driven mods, the meta of how to optimally play will inevitably shift. For now, understanding the consequences of your quest choices, securing your economy, and mastering the tight combat mechanics of encounters like Bakir's Maze remain the true test of your skills.
