The Blood of Dawnwalker isn't a power fantasy about being a cool vampire. Its story is built on a much more human hook: a young man trying to save his family, who gets cursed with a power he never asked for. Here's the full setup — spoiler-free for anything beyond the game's announced premise.

## The world: Vale Sangora, AD 1347

The game takes place in **1347**, in a fictional valley called **Vale Sangora**, set "somewhere in the Carpathians" in Central/Southeastern Europe. It's a grim moment in history: plague is spreading through the population just as an ancient evil tightens its grip. The vampire lord who rules the valley levies a **blood tax** on the living, and the humans beneath him are sliding into servitude and despair.

This is folklore-flavored dark fantasy — closer to Eastern-European vampire myth than the gothic-romance vampire of most games.

## Coen: the reluctant Dawnwalker

You play **Coen**, born to a peasant family as the **eldest of four children**. He started working the **silver mines** alongside his father as a boy. He dreamed of leaving the valley for adventure, but never could — his family needed him, and he wouldn't abandon them.

That mining past becomes the pivot of the whole story. Years of exposure gave Coen **silver poisoning**, and when the vampire lord **Brencis** tries to turn him, the silver in his blood stops the transformation from completing. The result: Coen becomes a **Dawnwalker** — fully human by day, vampire by night, belonging to neither world.

## The family at the heart of it

Coen's motivation is personal:

- **Lunka** — his little sister, who is **stricken with the plague**.
- **Esme** — his mother.

Brencis **saves Lunka with his blood**, then immediately turns that mercy into leverage, effectively holding Coen's family hostage. Coen is left with roughly **30 days** to save them. If you're worried that means a stressful real-time clock, the director has publicly reassured players it isn't a punishing countdown — we cover exactly how it works in our [30-day time limit explainer](/guides/30-day-time-limit-explained/).

## Brencis: an ancient, Roman vampire

The antagonist, **Brencis**, was born in the **Roman Empire in AD 131** as **Caeso Burrienus Laurentius**, into a wealthy noble family. Over more than a thousand years he became the formidable Lord of Vampires who now rules Vale Sangora. He commands a circle of vampire officers, giving the game a roster of memorable, dangerous foes rather than a single final boss.

## Why the setup works

The genius of the premise is that it ties the **mechanics to the emotion**: you're human by day because Coen *is* still human, clinging to his family; you're a monster by night because that's the price of the power he needs to save them. Every choice about when and how to use that power carries narrative weight.
