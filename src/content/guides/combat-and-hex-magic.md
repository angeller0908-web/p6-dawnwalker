By day, Coen is human — and Rebel Wolves has said it set out to build a swordplay system that could **"set a new standard for RPGs."** Daytime combat is deliberate, defensive, and skill-based, the polar opposite of the [nighttime predator](/guides/vampire-powers-and-wolf-form/). Here's how it works and how to master it.

## Four-directional blocking and parrying

The heart of daytime combat is **directional commitment.** You choose one of **four directions** to attack or to block and parry. That makes fights a duel of reads and timing rather than a button-mash:

- **Block in the right direction** to absorb an incoming blow.
- **Parry with precise timing** to open the enemy up.
- **Attack from the direction your foe isn't defending** to land clean hits.

Against skilled human opponents, sloppy swinging gets punished. Patience and reading your enemy win fights.

## Activation charges: your resource

As you **deal damage, you build activation charges.** These are the fuel for your most powerful options, so combat becomes a rhythm: land clean hits to bank charges, then spend them at the right moment.

## Hex magic

Spend charges on **Hex magic** — powerful spells that supplement your blade. Hexes give human Coen a burst of offensive power that a pure swordsman wouldn't have, and they're a major reason to invest in the magical side of your [skill trees](/guides/builds-and-skill-trees/).

## Executions

The other use for charges is **executions** — finishing moves that deal devastating damage. Save them for staggered or weakened enemies to end dangerous fights quickly. Aggressive, well-timed execution play can turn a tough duel in your favor.

## Putting it together

A strong daytime loop looks like this:

1. **Read** your opponent and defend with directional blocks/parries.
2. **Land clean, directional attacks** to bank activation charges.
3. **Spend charges** on a Hex to break the enemy's rhythm — or an execution to finish them.

How far you lean into Hex magic versus raw swordsmanship is a build choice.
